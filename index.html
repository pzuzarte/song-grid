        Song Grid help

        Arranges the patterns you've made in the grid editors into a song, and plays them together on your instruments. Song Grid makes no sound itself: it tells each open grid which pattern to play, and when. It needs a current Chrome or Edge. Your songs stay in your browser.

        Visits to this page are counted anonymously with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies.

        Set up

        1. Open the grids you want in the same browser, in other tabs: S-1 Grid, Protein Grid, Bass Station Grid, MPC Grid and TD-3 Grid.
        2. In each, connect its instrument, and tick Sync. The badge at the top of this page shows which grids are linked. Rows for grids that aren't open are greyed.

        Parts

        • A song is made of parts, such as Intro, Verse or Chorus, and the Arranger puts them in the order they play. Switch between the two with the tabs, or Tab.
        • On the Parts tab each column is a part. Click its name to change it, and its colour dot to change the colour of its blocks in the Arranger. A part that isn't in the Arranger yet has its name in grey.
        • Bars sets how long the part lasts.
        • Each row is a grid. Choose the pattern it plays in that part, from the patterns kept in the song's Assets, by name, or Off for silence. Add patterns to the assets first, from the grid or from files. A pattern number the song plays with nothing kept for it shows as "(not in Assets)". A pattern shorter than its part repeats, and a longer one is cut at the part's end.
        • TD-3 Grid edits one pattern at a time, so its row offers Grid pattern, whatever is in TD-3 Grid when the song plays, and the patterns saved in its library, by name. A library pattern plays without changing the grid. Its library is kept in its library folder, so every pattern saved there is listed.
        • Bars are counted in 4/4. An MPC sequence in another time signature still restarts at each part.
        • Every grid starts its pattern from the first step at the start of each part, so they stay together even when their patterns are different lengths.
        • The buttons above each part move it left or right, copy it, or delete it. Their order on this tab is only for editing. Copy and Add part put the new part at the end of the Arranger too. Deleting a part removes its blocks from the Arranger.
        • Tempo, fills, presets shows more for each part. Tempo plays it at its own BPM, or leave it empty for the song's. Transpose shifts the notes of every grid but MPC Grid. Each grid's Fill plays in the part's last bar in place of its pattern, or Off for a gap, and needs a part of two bars or more. Preset, for S-1, Protein and Bass Station, sends a program change to pick that preset as the part starts.
        • Warnings under the table point out patterns a part plays that aren't kept with the song, once some are kept from that grid, and TD-3 patterns missing from its library. Their cells are marked.

        Arranger

        • The Arranger tab lays the song out left to right as blocks, each a part in its colour, as wide as its bars. A part can be in it any number of times. The ruler above shows the bars, and the time each starts at, and the top of the tab shows how long the song is.
        • Click a part in the row of parts at the top to add it at the end, or drag it to where it should go: a line shows where it will land.
        • Drag a block to move it. Hold Alt (Option) as you drop it to copy it instead.
        • Click a block to pick it. Duplicate, or D, adds a copy after it, and Delete, or the Delete key, removes it. The part stays on the Parts tab. ← and → pick the block before or after. Double-click a block to edit its part.
        • − and + zoom out and in, and Fit shows the whole song. The −, + and 0 keys do the same, and so does Ctrl or Cmd with the scroll wheel, or a pinch.
        • Under the arranger, Playing shows what each grid plays in the block playing and the next two, as the song will go, with its loops and what's up next. Before Play it shows how the song starts, or how it goes on from the block picked.
        • While the song plays, a line shows where it is. Play next plays the block picked when the one playing finishes.
        • Songs made before the Arranger play their parts once each, in order, as they did.

        Playing

        • Play starts every linked grid at the song's BPM, and Stop stops them. Space does both. Stop on a grid stops them all too.
        • The part playing is highlighted. At the end the grids stop, unless Loop is on.
        • Changing the BPM while it plays changes it on every grid.
        • Under each part, the loop button (∞) makes the part repeat each time it's reached, until you click it again. It turns orange while it's on, and it's saved with the song. You can turn it on or off while the song plays, and every grid changes on the next bar.
        • The up next button (») plays that part, from its next block in the Arranger, when the block playing finishes its run through, even if it's looping. It stays orange until then, and clicking it again cancels it.
        • The bar under the part playing fills as it plays, with the bar it's on beside it. ◀ Part and Part ▶, at the top, jump to the start of the block before or after, on the next bar.
        • Changes to the parts or the Arranger while it plays take effect the next time you press Play.
        • M beside a grid mutes it, and S solos it, muting every other grid. With any soloed, only those play. They work while the song plays, on the next bar, and stay as they are when you press Play.
        • Count-in clicks for a bar on this computer before the song starts.
        • Keys while it plays: 1 to 9 play that block in the Arranger next, L loops the part playing or lets it go, and ← and → jump a block back or forward.
        • While a song plays, the grids switch patterns themselves. Pressing Play on a grid plays it on its own as usual, without the song.

        Assets

        • The Assets panel keeps the patterns and sounds this song uses, with the song, so they're in one place and saved with it, in its backups too.
        • Add file…, beside a grid, opens the Mac's Open dialog to choose pattern files on this computer: S-1 patch files, Protein Grid and Bass Station Grid pattern files, or TD-3 .syx files. Choose several at once if you like. The grid reads them as it would its own, so they come with their names, and the Bass Station's and Protein's with their sounds, and they're kept with the song. TD-3 patterns are added to its library too. The grid needs to be open with Sync ticked. It remembers the last folder you used for each grid.
        • Add, beside a grid, lists what it has: the patterns in it now, the patterns saved in its library, and sounds. Tick the ones this song needs, choose a sound if you want one, and press Add to song. Search, and ★ Favourites, narrow a long library.
        • What each offers: S-1 Grid, its patterns and the patches in its library, as patterns and as sounds. Protein Grid and Bass Station Grid, their patterns, the patterns saved in their library folder, and the sounds in their sound library. MPC Grid, the sequences in the open project. TD-3 Grid, its grid pattern and its library. A grid needs its library folder open to list it: Choose library folder, or Allow library folder, is beside Save in each grid.
        • A song keeps one sound for each grid: the one you pick replaces the one it had. Protein Grid reads only the settings you've mapped from a sound, so click Teach reading on its Synth tab once first. The TD-3 and the MPC have no sound to keep: the TD-3's is set by its knobs, and the MPC's samples stay in its project.
        • Each pattern kept goes in a pattern number, shown beside it. One from a grid keeps its own. One from a library goes in the first number the arrangement plays that has nothing kept for it, or else the first free one. Choose another number to move it, which swaps it with the pattern there.
        • Collect all copies everything in every linked grid at once: each pattern with notes or knob movements, and the sound in its controls, or the one the Bass Station is playing. It replaces what was kept from each grid before.
        • Patterns the arrangement plays are marked Used. Remove anything the song doesn't need with ×.
        • Load all puts the song's assets into every linked grid, Load beside a grid puts in just its own, and ↑ beside a pattern or sound puts in just that one. Use it after opening a song, so each grid has the patterns the song needs.
        • Each pattern goes into its pattern number, replacing what's there. Other slots aren't touched. Undo in the grid brings back what was there. In MPC Grid each sequence is undone separately.
        • MPC Grid puts its sequences back into the open project, by number, on the track being edited. They reach the MPC when you save there. TD-3 Grid puts its grid pattern back in the grid, and adds library patterns its library doesn't have.
        • Sounds are sent straight to the instruments: S-1 Grid's and Protein Grid's controls are set and sent, and the Bass Station's sound is sent through its editor. Undo doesn't bring a sound back, so save one you want to keep first.

        Takes and set lists

        • Rec records a take. Press it, then play: every block that plays, in order, with your jumps, loops and up nexts, is saved as a new song in Saved songs when the song stops or you press Rec again. Each pass of a looped part is its own block in the take's Arranger. R does the same.
        • The Set list plays songs one after another. Click + beside a saved song to add it, and the arrows to change the order. Play set opens each song, puts its assets into the grids, and plays it, then the next when it ends. A song with Loop on doesn't end, so turn Loop off for the set to go on.

        Saving

        • Export MIDI downloads the arrangement as a MIDI file to open in other music software: a track for each grid, made from the patterns kept with the song, with each part's tempo, transpose, fill and preset, in the Arranger's order. Each block plays once. Patterns that aren't kept are left silent, and the status lists them.
        • When you open a song with assets, Song Grid offers to load them into the grids.
        • Save keeps the song in this browser under its name, and Save as new keeps a copy. Click a saved song to open it.
        • Songs point to pattern numbers in each grid, so changing a grid's pattern changes the song too.
        • Back up downloads every saved song as a file, and Restore adds songs from one.
